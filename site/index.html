Switch users in one keystroke.

On Windows, switching to another account means opening Start, clicking your picture and finding the right name. Userhop puts every account on the PC in a small panel that opens with Win Shift U. Pick one and Windows takes it from there. It runs on macOS and Linux too.

Try it here

Click the Userhop icon in the taskbar or press U. Then use the arrow keys, Enter and Esc. This is the real app interface with made-up accounts; nothing leaves this page.

Userhop's job ends at this screen. It never sees what is typed here.

What happens when you pick someone

Windows 10 and 11
Userhop asks Windows to open sign-in for that account. If Windows can't preselect it, the PC locks and you choose the account in the bottom-left corner. Everything you had open keeps running.
macOS 11 and later
The macOS login window appears, the same one Fast User Switching uses. Your session stays as you left it.
Linux
Your session locks, then GDM, LightDM or SDDM shows its login screen. Wayland desktops don't allow global shortcuts, so add a custom one that runs userhop --toggle.

What it won't do

  • Ask for a password. Not yours, not anyone else's. Signing in always happens on your operating system's own screen.
  • Open another person's desktop. Their session only appears after the OS has asked them to sign in.
  • Phone home. No analytics, no update checks, no network requests at all.
  • Keep a record of who you switched to. The last account lives in memory for Switch back and is forgotten when the app quits. Only your theme and shortcut are saved.
  • Need admin rights. It runs as you, with no background service and nothing added to the login process.
  • Pretend. When switching isn't possible, like inside a remote desktop session, the panel says so instead of failing quietly.

Questions

Will I lose my open apps when I switch?

No. Switching never signs you out. Your apps are where you left them when you come back.

Why do I sometimes have to pick the account again on Windows?

Preselecting the account relies on an internal Windows component. When it isn't available, Userhop locks the PC and you choose the account on the sign-in screen. Windows handles the password either way.

How do I switch back?

Userhop lists the account you last switched to first and selects it, so the shortcut followed by Enter takes you there again. The tray menu also has Switch back to ….

Does it collect anything?

No. There are no accounts, analytics or update checks. This page doesn't use cookies or trackers either.
