Every account on your computer, one keystroke away.

Userhop is a tiny tray and menu bar app for computers shared between work and personal accounts, or with family. Press Win + Shift + U, pick an account, and your operating system signs you in. The app never sees a password.

Small app, no detours

Normally switching users means digging through the Start menu, Control Center or a power menu. Userhop puts every account in a compact panel inspired by Windows 11.

  • Instant

    One shortcut opens the panel next to your taskbar or menu bar. Arrow keys, Enter and Esc do the rest.

  • Honest status

    See who is signed in, who is signed in remotely and who is not signed in. It never claims a switch your OS hasn't finished.

  • Your OS stays in charge

    Windows, the macOS login window or your Linux login screen does the signing in, exactly as it normally would.

  • Switch back fast

    The account you used last is listed first and already selected, so the shortcut followed by Enter takes you back.

  • Native look

    Acrylic glass on Windows 11, vibrancy on macOS, light and dark themes, high contrast and reduced motion.

  • Lightweight

    A ~1.3 MB Windows installer. It sits quietly in the tray and uses almost no CPU when idle.

How it works

  1. 1

    Open the panel

    Press the shortcut or click the tray icon.

  2. 2

    Pick an account

    With the arrow keys and Enter, or a click.

  3. 3

    Sign in on your OS

    Your system's own sign-in screen takes over. Your apps keep running.

Windows 10 and 11

Userhop asks Windows to open sign-in for the selected account. If that isn't available, the PC locks and you pick the account in the bottom-left corner.

macOS 11 and later

The familiar macOS login window appears, just like Fast User Switching. Your session keeps running.

Linux

Your session locks first, then GDM, LightDM or SDDM opens. On Wayland, add a custom shortcut that runs userhop --toggle.

A closer look

Private by design

An account switcher is close to your sign-in, so Userhop does as little as possible and leaves authentication to your operating system.

  • Never asks for a password. No passwords, PINs or tokens are requested, stored or passed on.
  • No direct access to other desktops. Another session only opens after your OS asks that person to sign in.
  • Nothing leaves your computer. No accounts, analytics, ads, update pings or network requests.
  • Almost nothing is saved. Only your preferences. The last used account is kept in memory and forgotten when the app quits.
  • No admin rights, no services. It runs as you and adds nothing to the login process.

Questions

Will I lose my open apps when I switch?

No. Switching never signs you out. When you come back to your account, everything is where you left it.

Why do I sometimes have to pick the account again on Windows?

Preselecting the account depends on an internal Windows component. If it isn't available, Userhop locks the PC and you choose the account on the Windows sign-in screen. Windows handles authentication in either case.

Does it work in a remote desktop session?

No. Switching users is a feature of the physical screen, so it isn't offered inside a remote session. The panel tells you when that's the case.

The shortcut doesn't work on Linux.

Apps can't register global shortcuts on Wayland. Add a custom shortcut in your desktop's keyboard settings that runs userhop --toggle.

Is it free?

Yes. Userhop is open source under the MIT License. The code is on GitHub.
